Which model and layer processes negation?

Concern: ../meta-audit.md lines 190–196 state "Layer 10 :: Gemma-2-27B negation operator", with ablation collapsing downstream representations ~40%, attributed to "Zhou et al., ICML 2026". Does a paper support that row?

Status: NO. Every element of the row is unattributable as written. The matching paper tests different models, and neither the layer number nor the percentage is in it.

the row, verbatim from the audit

- Layer 10 :: Gemma-2-27B negation operator
- Acts as causal trigger → ablating it collapses downstream representations ~40%
- Construction mechanism dominates: mid-layer attention builds ¬Y representation
- Suppression coexists, weaker
- Late-layer shortcut h...

The third and fourth bullets match a real paper. The first, second and model name do not.

what the matching paper is

arXiv:2605.03052 — How Language Models Process Negation, Zhou, Zhejian; Zhou, Tianyi; Jia, Robin; May, Jonathan, 2026-05-04, rev. 2026-05-29, cs.CL. PDF in ../raw/. Verified by curl against the arXiv citation_author and citation_abstract meta tags.

Its abstract states:

We apply a range of observational and causal interpretability techniques on Mistral-7B and Llama-3.1-8B to show that models implement both mechanisms, with the "constructive" mechanism being more prominent.

And, on the failure side:

Their poor accuracy is due to late-layer attention behavior that promotes simple shortcuts; ablating those attention modules greatly improves accuracy.

the compound error

Three separate faults stacked in one row:

  1. a different model family named than the one measured
  2. a layer number with no source
  3. a percentage of representation collapse substituted for the paper's actual finding, which is an accuracy improvement after ablation

Fault 3 is the one worth dwelling on. "Collapses downstream representations ~40%" and "greatly improves accuracy" are opposite-signed claims about what ablation does. The first says the circuit is load-bearing; the second says removing a shortcut helps. A row that inverts the sign of its source is not a citation error that a reader can catch by checking the venue.

what to do

Either locate a Gemma-2-27B paper and cite it for the layer-10/40% row, or rewrite the row to the Mistral/Llama result: construction-dominant negation in mid-layer attention, with late-layer shortcut attention causing the errors. The second is supported and the audit's argument survives on it — the audit's mechanism section does not need Gemma or layer 10.
