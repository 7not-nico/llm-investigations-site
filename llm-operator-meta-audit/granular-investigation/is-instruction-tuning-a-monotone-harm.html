Is instruction tuning a monotone harm to operator fidelity?

Is instruction tuning a monotone harm to operator fidelity?

Concern: the audit's fundamentals table states "Instruction-tuning destroys operator comprehension: MetaGlyph mid-sized IT models (7–12B): 0% fidelity vs base (3B): 33%". Does the source support a monotone claim?

Status: NO — the relationship is explicitly non-monotonic, and the audit drops the half of the finding that reverses the conclusion.

the source

arXiv:2601.07354 — Semantic Compression of LLM Instructions via Symbolic Metalanguages (MetaGlyph), van Gassen, Ernst (single author, Independent Researcher), 2026-01-12. PDF in ../raw/. Verified by curl against the arXiv citation_* meta tags.

Membership operator (∈) fidelity, from the paper's table:

model scale ∈ fidelity
OLMo 7B 0.0%
Gemma 12B 0.0%
Qwen 7B 20.4%
Llama 3B 33.3%
Kimi K2 — 36.0%
Gemini 2.5 Flash — 49.9%
Claude Haiku 4.5 — 26.0%
GPT-5.2 Chat — 91.3%

∩ (intersection): 0.0% for Llama, Qwen, OLMo, Gemma and Kimi; 2.7% Gemini; 1.5% Claude; 21.4% GPT-5.2 Chat. Constraint composition fails across essentially all models.

The paper's reading: "Mid-sized open-source models (7B–12B) show near-zero operator fidelity, suggesting a U-shaped relationship where sufficient scale overcomes instruction-tuning biases." It also reports GPT-5.2 Chat recovering the highest membership fidelity observed, and Gemma 12B reaching 100% JSON parse success against Llama 3B's 30%.

what the audit's phrasing drops

Read as "small instruction-tuned models are worst", the row is fair and the numbers are exact. Read as "instruction tuning degrades comprehension", it is not what the paper says, because:

  • GPT-5.2 Chat — instruction-tuned by any reasonable reading — posts 91.3%, the highest in the set
  • the author explicitly calls the relationship U-shaped, i.e. it recovers
  • parse success rises with scale (Gemma 12B 100% vs Llama 3B 30%) even where operator fidelity does not

The audit's own framing elsewhere is "instruction-tuning degrades operator comprehension", phrased as a general harm. The source's framing is a harm that scale overcomes.

why the distinction is load-bearing here

This audit's operational claim is that operator fidelity is model-specific, not universal — and that part holds well. But the specific mechanism offered is "instruction tuning is the culprit", and that mechanism is the part the source contradicts at the top of the range.

For this workspace the corrected reading is the more useful one: the models in play here are small and open, which places them in the 0–20% band, not the ambiguous middle. The audit reaches a right operational conclusion for its own models through an over-general premise.

also worth noting

The audit's row cites MetaGlyph for "∩ fails 0–21% across ALL models". Verified: the range is 0.0%–21.4%, and "all models" holds. This row is clean.

what to do

Keep the number, qualify the mechanism: instruction-tuned mid-size open models show near-zero operator fidelity, with the relationship U-shaped and instruction-tuned frontier models recovering. That preserves the audit's operational point and drops the false generalisation.
