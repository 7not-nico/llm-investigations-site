Do determiners recruit the most shared units?

Do determiners recruit the most shared units?

Concern: the audit's tier-1 argument leans on determiner-noun agreement's 68.91% overlap — the highest of the three agreement types — to conclude that determiners are strongly represented, which bears on why a clause-initial no X escapes a function-word detector. Does the number support that?

Status: the number is real and correctly quoted. It measures a different quantity than the argument needs, so the inference is a hypothesis, not a result.

the source

arXiv:2512.03676 — Different types of syntactic agreement recruit the same units within large language models, Kryvosheieva, Daria; de Varda, Andrea; Fedorenko, Evelina; Tuckute, Greta. v1 2025-12-03, rev. 2026-04-11, cs.CL. PDF in ../raw/. Full text read with pdftotext; the passage below is from the Results.

what the paper says, verbatim

The results are shown in Figure 4: across most phenomena, the within-category overlaps are reliably higher than the cross-category overlaps (t = 2.07, p = 0.0312 via a two-sample Welch t-test). Interestingly, this pattern appears to be driven by the three agreement categories: determiner-noun agreement, subject-verb agreement, and anaphor agreement (average within-category overlaps: 68.91%, 40.29%, and 23.10%, respectively).

Separately: overlap with other agreement phenomena runs 15.26%–23.17%, against 5.72%–8.23% for non-agreement phenomena.

the fault is the word "within"

The three figures are within-category overlaps — how much the same units recur across instances of one agreement type. The audit's phrasing, "agreement categories (subject-verb: 40.29%, anaphor: 23.10%, determiner-noun: 68.91%) recruit overlapping units", reads as overlap between the categories. Different quantities.

The statistic that carries the paper's finding is the within-versus-cross contrast, and the paper reports the three numbers as drivers of that contrast (t = 2.07, p = 0.0312) — not as a ranking of agreement types by importance.

why it matters for this argument

The inference being made is roughly: determiners are represented most strongly, so no X is processed by the strongest functional machinery, so a detector reading function-word-initial clauses misses it in a principled way.

That inference needs cross-category overlap — specifically, that determiner units are shared with, or recruit, units serving other constructions. The paper measures cross-category agreement overlap at 15.26%–23.17%, a single pooled band, without a per-type breakdown in the abstract or this passage.

So the strong reading is not supported by this row. It is not refuted either. The paper is compatible with it. That makes it a probe worth running, not a citation to lean on.

what would settle it

Read the paper's per-type cross-category overlap matrix (Figures 3–4 and appendices) for the determiner-noun category specifically. If determiner units overlap substantially more with other categories than subject-verb units do, the audit's inference is supported by data already collected. If the matrix pools them, the inference needs a new measurement.

That is a read of an existing figure, not an experiment — cheap, and it either upgrades the argument or downgrades it to a hypothesis.
