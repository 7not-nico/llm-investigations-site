Are function-word heads a general LLM finding, or a GPT-2 result?

Are function-word heads a general LLM finding, or a GPT-2 result?

Concern: the audit's tier-1 core states "Function words (DET, ADP, CCONJ, SCONJ, AUX): specialized heads in layers 3–4 (ACL 2026)". Which models does that hold for?

Status: the claim is accurate and precisely stated in the source — and the source's subject is GPT-2 variants trained on counterfactual corpora, not LLMs generally.

the source

arXiv:2601.21191 — Function Words as Statistical Cues for Language Learning, Yang, Xiulin; Getz, Heidi; Wilcox, Ethan Gotlieb. v1 2026-01-29, rev. 2026-04-20, cs.CL, Comments: ACL 2026. PDF in ../raw/.

what the paper does

Three parts, and the third is the one the audit cites:

  1. Cross-linguistic corpus analysis of 186 languages, confirming three distributional properties of function words are universal: high frequency, reliable syntactic association, phrase-boundary alignment.
  2. Counterfactual language modeling + ablation on English, establishing a Goldilocks effect — function words must be frequent enough to be reliable yet diverse enough to remain informative to structural dependency.
  3. Attention probing and function-word ablation, the RQ3 results.

The RQ3 result, from the full text:

Models trained with NATURALFUNCTION exhibit a highly concentrated pattern of function word attention: a small number of heads, primarily in Layers 3 and 4, account for the majority of function word attention across BLiMP categories. This pattern indicates the emergence of specialized representations tied to function words when all three distributional properties are present.

Conditions disrupting any of the three properties — RANDOMDEP, BIGRAMDEP, WITHINBOUNDARY — fail to produce the concentrated pattern. Models trained without function words are excluded from the analysis.

two scope facts the audit drops

The models are GPT-2 variants, trained on the paper's own counterfactual language variants. Not the seven open-weight models in the sibling agreement paper, and not a modern LLM. The paper does use BERT in separate experiments, but the layer-3/4 result is the GPT-2 probing run.

The heads are conditional on the corpus. The paper's own conclusion: "Disrupting any of the three properties prevents the emergence of focused function word attention heads." So "function words get dedicated heads in layers 3–4" is a statement about a learner trained on a well-formed corpus, with the counterfactual arms as the control that establishes the condition.

why the narrower subject is the more interesting claim

The conditional form is the finding, not a caveat on it. The paper is not reporting that function words are represented in layers 3–4 — it is reporting that the corpus's distributional properties cause that representation to emerge, demonstrated by building languages where the properties are absent and watching the heads fail to concentrate.

That is a stronger claim than the audit uses it for, and it is also a different kind of claim from what the audit's tier table rests on. See the next note.

how I got this wrong first

Abstract-only checking found no layer numbers, no attention heads, and a corpus-statistics framing — and I recorded the audit's row as REFUTED. That was wrong. The claim is stated nearly verbatim in the Results. The lesson is specific: a paper whose headline contribution is a corpus analysis can bury a mechanistic result in RQ3, and an abstract that omits it is not evidence of absence.

what to do

Keep the row, and qualify the subject: GPT-2 variants trained on controlled corpora, with the heads emerging as a function of the corpus's distributional properties. Attribute the conditional to the paper rather than restating it as unconditional.
