Do the two function-word findings measure the same thing?

Do the two function-word findings measure the same thing?

Concern: the audit's tier-1 synthesis stacks a corpus finding and a mechanistic finding into one causal story about why syntax outranks semantics. Does that composition hold?

Status: NO — they are two independent measurements, and the audit's causal reading rests on a pairing no source made.

the two findings

Corpus-side (arXiv:2601.21191, Yang, Getz, Wilcox): across 186 languages, function words have high frequency, reliable syntactic association, and phrase-boundary alignment — all three universal. Preserving these properties in English training data facilitates acquisition, with a Goldilocks effect.

Model-side (same paper): in GPT-2 variants trained on that data, a small number of attention heads primarily in layers 3–4 account for most function-word attention, and this concentration fails when any of the three properties is disrupted.

what the audit builds on them

../meta-audit.md, tier 1:

Function words (DET, ADP, CCONJ, SCONJ, AUX): specialized heads in layers 3-4 (ACL 2026) Function words balance frequency (reliability) → diversity (informativeness) — Goldilocks effect

and the tier header asserts syntax is the highest-weight grammatical category, which then places function words at the top of the weight sort.

the seam

Read carefully, the paper's two results are connected — part (3) varies the corpus properties and observes the head concentration appear and disappear. So the paper itself establishes that corpus properties cause the representation, within GPT-2 variants on controlled data.

What the paper does not do is compare function-word weight against content-word weight. There is no measurement here that function words are the highest-weighted grammatical category. That ranking comes from the audit's tier table, which also draws on:

  • arXiv:2512.03676 (Kryvosheieva et al.) — agreement-unit overlap
  • LREC 2024 (Jang, Byun, Shin) — content words attended for semantic tasks, function words for syntactic tasks
  • CoNLL 2026 embedding-geometry work — closed-class words occupy lower-dimensional subspaces

Those are three more subjects (seven open-weight models, BERT, embedding geometry) and none of them measures a weight sort. The tier-1 heading is a synthesis across four papers on four subjects, presented in the table's flat voice alongside rows that are single-paper measurements.

why the synthesis is not free

Two claims are being merged:

  1. Descriptive: function words have concentrated early-layer representations, and syntactic tasks attend them more than semantic tasks do.
  2. Normative: therefore syntax is the highest-weight grammatical category.

(2) needs a common scale across constructions — subjects, objects, predicates, gerunds, clauses — to be a weight sort at all. No cited paper provides one. The audit gets to a ranking without ever putting the ranked things on one axis.

the irony worth stating

The tier table's own ranking places syntax above semantics, and its support for that comes from two rows whose subjects are an encoder (BERT, LREC 2024) and GPT-2 variants (function words), plus a set of open-weight generative models (agreement). The claim about what modern generative LLMs represent is assembled from encoder and small-model evidence, and the audit does not mark the seam.

what to do

Split the tier-1 heading into two rows: what each cited paper measured, on which subject; and separately, the audit's synthesis, marked as a synthesis. If the highest-weight ranking is load-bearing, it needs one paper that measures weights on one axis across constructions.
