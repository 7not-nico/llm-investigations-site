arXiv:2512.03676 — Different types of syntactic agreement recruit the same units within large language models Kryvosheieva, Daria; de Varda, Andrea; Fedorenko, Evelina; Tuckute, Greta paper, cs.CL v1 2025-12-03, rev. 2026-04-11. arXiv:2601.21191 — Function Words as Statistical Cues for Language Learning Yang, Xiulin; Getz, Heidi; Wilcox, Ethan Gotlieb paper, cs.CL, Comments: ACL 2026 v1 2026-01-29, rev. 2026-04-20. Cross-linguistic corpus analysis of 186 languages confirming three distributional properties of function words: high frequency, reliable syntactic association, phrase-boundary alignment. Counterfactual language modeling + ablation on English: preserving these properties facilitates acquisition, with a Goldilocks effect — frequent enough to be reliable, diverse enough to stay informative. Probing shows learning conditions produce systematically different reliance on function words.

Citation corrections found while resolving

REFUTED — the Skean et al. attribution. meta-audit.md cites "Where meaning lives: Layer-wise accessibility" to Skean et al. The paper by that title is arXiv:2601.03798, by Tikhomirova & Wulff. "Skean et al." is a different paper — arXiv:2502.02013, Layer by Layer: Uncovering Hidden Representations in Language Models. The audit's row merges two distinct works: it takes the title and finding from one and the authors from the other.

The finding survives — Tikhomirova & Wulff do report that the final layer is rarely optimal for psycholinguistic features, and that layer-wise accessibility differs between encoder and decoder models. What is wrong is the attribution, which means the row currently points a reader at a paper that does not contain its claim.

Numeric drift, same direction. Emergence and Localisation of Semantic Role Circuits (arXiv:2511.20910, Aljaafari, Carvalho, Freitas) reports 89–94% within 28 nodes and 24–59% component overlap. The audit's table says 89–92% and 24–51%. The finding is the same; the ranges have narrowed at both ends. Neither end is load-bearing for the audit's argument, but a table whose numbers drift from its sources cannot be trusted without re-derivation.

Overstated — "NP-carries-roles, not verbs." The signal is coming from inside the noun phrase! (DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.scil-main.40, Li, Lynch, van Schijndel) supports the noun phrase as the carrier of proto-role information. The audit's "not verbs" is stronger than the paper's position, which is that NPs are at least as informative as verbs.

Verification verdict

VERIFIED — the agreement-overlap claim. The audit's tier-1 row is "Kryvosheieva, ACL 2026, agreement categories recruit overlapping units." The paper is real, the authors match, and the overlapping-units finding is its stated headline. The venue is arXiv preprint with a later revision; the audit's "ACL 2026" venue label was not visible on the abs page, so treat the venue as unconfirmed until a proceedings record is found.

VERIFIED IN FULL TEXT, MISREAD IF TAKEN AS A CROSS-CATEGORY CLAIM — the 68.91% / 40.29% / 23.10% overlap percentages. The figures are real and appear in the Results (p. around the Figure 4 discussion): "this pattern appears to be driven by the three agreement categories: determiner-noun agreement, subject-verb agreement, and anaphor agreement (average within-category overlaps: 68.91%, 40.29%, and 23.10%, respectively)."

Two things must travel with the numbers. First, they are within-category overlaps — how much units recur across instances of the same agreement type — not how much determiner-noun units overlap subject-verb units. The audit's phrasing ("agreement categories … recruit overlapping units") reads as the latter. Second, the statistic that carries the finding is the within vs cross contrast (t = 2.07, p = 0.0312, Welch), and the paper reports the three numbers as drivers of that contrast, not as a ranking of agreement types by importance. Separately, the paper measures cross-category overlap with agreement phenomena at 15.26%–23.17% versus 5.72%–8.23% for non-agreement phenomena.

So the determiners-are-strongly-represented inference is suggested by a within-category statistic, which is not the quantity that claim needs. That makes it a hypothesis, not a measured result — and it is exactly the load-bearing step in the audit's argument about no X.

VERIFIED IN FULL TEXT — "specialized attention heads for function words in layers 3–4". The claim is accurate and precisely stated. The abstract omits it (a corpus-statistics framing: 186-language analysis plus counterfactual language modeling and ablations), but the Results carry it: "Models trained with NATURAL FUNCTION exhibit a highly concentrated pattern of function word attention: a small number of heads, primarily in Layers 3 and 4, account for the majority of function word attention across BLiMP categories." Conditions disrupting any of the three distributional properties — random dependency, bigram dependency, within-boundary — fail to produce the concentrated pattern; so do models trained without function words.

Two scope facts the audit omits and should not drop. The models are GPT-2 variants trained on the paper's own counterfactual language variants, not the seven open-weight models of the agreement paper or a modern LLM. And the heads are found by an attention probe, with the finding tied to the presence of the three distributional properties in the training corpus. "Function words get dedicated heads in layers 3–4" is therefore a statement about a learner trained on a well-formed corpus, which is a narrower subject than "in LLMs".

VERIFIED — the LREC 2024 claim, with the model named. Jang/Byun/Shin confirm the content-word / function-word attention split by task type. Note the subject is BERT, an encoder — not a generative LLM, and not one of the seven open-weight models in the agreement paper. The two verified papers therefore cover two different architectures, which is worth stating wherever the finding is reused.

MECHANISM GAP — the audit's tier table mixes two kinds of claim. "Function words get dedicated heads in layers 3–4" is a claim about representation inside a model. "Function words are frequent, reliably associated, and boundary-aligned across 186 languages" is a claim about corpora that explains why a learner would build such a circuit. Neither measured the other, and the audit's tier ranking ("syntax is the highest-weight grammatical category") rests on the pair being one result rather than two.
