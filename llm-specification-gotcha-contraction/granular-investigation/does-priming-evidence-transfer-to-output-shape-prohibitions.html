Does the priming evidence transfer to output-shape prohibitions?

Does the priming evidence transfer to output-shape prohibitions?

Concern: the audit's tier table treats one class of negative-instruction failure as covering both named-token bans and style/output-shape prohibitions. Does the evidence transfer?

Status: NO — the mechanism does not reach the shape case, and the transfer is the audit's own inference, not any paper's claim.

what was checked

arXiv:2601.08070 — Semantic Gravity Wells: Why Negative Constraints Backfire, Rana, Shailesh (single author), 2026-01-12, cs.AI, 10pp/8 figures, code at github.com/gut-puncture. Metadata and abstract read from the arXiv citation_* meta tags; PDF in ../raw/.

what the paper measures

The paper opens by defining its own intervention: "Negative constraints (instructions of the form 'do not use word X')". Every headline number sits inside that scope.

  • semantic pressure → violation probability: p = σ(-2.40 + 2.27·P₀)
  • n = 40,000 samples; bootstrap 95% CI for the slope [2.21, 2.33]
  • suppression is present but weaker in failures: −5.2pp vs −22.8pp, a 4.4× asymmetry
  • priming failure 87.5% of violations; override failure 12.5%, with +0.39 late-layer FFN contribution — nearly 4× larger than in successes
  • layers 23–27 causally responsible; activation patching there flips the sign

why the scope does not transfer

The stated mechanism is that naming a forbidden word activates it. That is a claim about the referent of the prohibition being a specific token with a well-defined probability.

no summary, no invented files, no extra reasoning — the forms this corpus is dominated by — do not name a token. They constrain an output shape. Two different operations:

named token unnamed shape
referent a word with a probability a property of the response
mechanism available activate-then-suppress none established here
paper's evidence direct, n=40,000 none

The large magnitude does not widen the mechanism. A 87.5% failure rate on token bans says nothing about whether output-shape prohibitions fail at 87.5%, at 5%, or at all.

what this does to the audit

Two rows in ../meta-audit.md inherit the 87.5% figure at the behavioural tier. They should carry the scope with them or lose the number. The mechanism they need does not currently exist in any verified source — see does-an-artifact-back-the-40-negative-threshold.md for how thin the tier-3 evidence actually is.

what would settle it

A probe that varies the prohibition's referent across both kinds — token ban versus output-shape constraint, matched for length and register — and measures violation rate for each. That is a two-arm test, not a literature question, and it is the same two-arm shape the eval-1 brief already proposes.
