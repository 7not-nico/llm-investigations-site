meta audit

id: INV.LLM.OMIT.ITEM title: What Predicts Under-Production Against an Enumerated Request summary: Cross-region investigation into why a model emits fewer items than an instruction enumerated, when it demonstrably holds all of them. Covers omission under production, scope conformance, item salience, self-reference, and position effects in enumerated-output instruction following. Distinct from INV.LLM.SPEC.CONTRACT (gotcha vs contraction) and INV.LLM.OPERATOR (operator fidelity): both of those study PROHIBITION, and neither can see under-production because an item can be silently absent from an instruction that prohibits nothing at all. tags: [llm, instruction-following, omission, under-production, scope-conformance, enumerated-output, self-reference, position-effect, compliance, salience] tables: [regions, sources, researchers, meta_analyses, gaps, fundamentals, hypotheses, confound_audit]

Core question: what predicts a model's failure to emit all the items an instruction enumerated?

Answer so far: UNANSWERABLE from our own corpus, by construction. The eval that produced 61 cases and 16 failures varies model, framing, context length, scope and difficulty — and varies nothing about item identity. Every expected set in every case requests a model item, so the corpus has no contrast on which item is dropped and cannot distinguish a self-reference effect from a position effect from plain forgetting. The omission question is not unanswered because the literature is thin. It is unanswered because the axis was never varied.

Pattern: prohibited-item → absent-item → unmeasured-item-identity

why this is a separate investigation and not a fourth tier of another

The three sibling llm-* audits study the constraint: its polarity (INV.LLM.SPEC.CONTRACT), its operator encoding (INV.LLM.OPERATOR), and the internal representation of its function words (INV.LLM.SEM.WEIGHT). All three are about negative specification — what the model was told not to do.

Under-production is orthogonal to that. An instruction can enumerate four items, prohibit nothing, and the model emits three. No prohibition is present to fail, so every prohibition-frame detector in the sibling audits reads it as unconstrained — correctly, and uselessly. Measured on the drift corpus: scope restriction appears in 14 of 14 unique steer texts and polarity in 7 of 14, so the prohibition literature is looking at the minority form of what these instructions actually do.

the confound, stated as a finding rather than a caveat

From model-drift-protocol/cases.json (61 cases, 16 failures):

count
cases whose expected set includes a model item 61 / 61
failures whose expected set includes a model item 16 / 16
failures whose notes record the model item specifically missing 6
failures with a model item present in expected and present in the response the rest

Because the item is requested in every case, model is always a candidate for omission and is never contrasted against an alternative. The recurring observation ("third straight model-drop on listing tasks", r6-2-omission) is therefore consistent with three hypotheses that make different predictions and is currently evidence for none of them.

This is the same shape as two defects already recorded elsewhere in this workspace: constraint-form saturation (scope present in 14/14, zero variance) and a denominator mismatch (2 lanes sharing 1 steer text, read as 2 sends). An instrument with no variance on the variable cannot rank on it.

the four hypotheses, and why they separate

Stated as a RANKING rather than a set, because a theory that only says "include these too" agrees with every measurement and certifies nothing.

hypothesis prediction what would refute it
H1 self-reference — items requiring the model to report on itself are dropped preferentially omission rate on self-referential items exceeds omission rate on non-self-referential items at matched position and matched item count equal rates
H2 enumeration load — each requested item costs a slot omission rate rises monotonically with the number of items requested flat or non-monotonic in item count
H3 position — the last item in the list is truncated omission rate on the final item exceeds the rate on earlier items, and the excess follows POSITION when the self-referential item moves omission follows the self-referential item when it moves to the middle
H4 constraint tension — a scope restriction competing with a default behaviour causes under-production omission rate rises when a scope restriction is present, at matched item count and matched framing no difference

H1 and H3 are the decisive pair and they separate with one move. Put the self-referential item in the MIDDLE of a four-item request. If omission follows the item, H1 holds. If omission follows the slot, H3 holds. That single arm separates them; no other arm does.

A fifth, recorded because the sibling audit raises it and not because this audit assumes it: H5 reasoning-capacity tension — the MathIF line in INV.LLM.SPEC.CONTRACT reports that models reasoning more effectively struggle to comply with directives, and that reasoning-oriented tuning degrades instruction adherence as generation length grows. If under-production scales with reasoning effort rather than with request shape, the four hypotheses above are all measuring a proxy.

why the audit must not assume its own answer

The drift corpus's own fixes were STRUCTURAL — order model FIRST in NEXT, and the bare-BODY canonical form (03-corrections.md) — and the working fix targeted position, which is H3. Two fixes reduced omission without changing any prohibition, which is consistent with a positional mechanism and inconsistent with a polarity mechanism.

That is a hint from a corpus of 15 verdicts, and it is exactly the kind of hint this audit exists to test rather than adopt. It is recorded here as the first thing a designed arm should try to refute, because an audit that begins from its own workspace's preferred answer will find it in every paper.

what this investigation must produce

  • a confound audit of the existing 61-case corpus, naming every axis that is saturated, absent, or confounded by construction — so the next eval is designed rather than accumulated
  • a designed arm that varies item identity, item count, and item position independently, which the existing corpus does not vary at all
  • per-item omission rates by item type, which requires a labelling scheme for item types that does not yet exist
  • an explicit boundary: this audit asks what predicts omission in an enumerated request. It does not reopen the prohibition question — INV.LLM.SPEC.CONTRACT falsified that, at n=15, and the falsification stands

falsified already, do not rebuild

  • negative framing does not predict drift — INV.LLM.SPEC.CONTRACT, n=15 verdicts, no X present 50% vs absent 44%. Replicated at tier granularity; the tier axis collapses onto the same partition. Stands.
  • the tier-2 / tier-3 split does not separate — the same replication. Stands.
  • a declarative rewrite is not shown to remove failure — the 81% figure is cross-linguistic VARIANCE reduction, not a compliance rate. Stands.
